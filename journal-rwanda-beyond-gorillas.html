Rwanda Beyond the Gorilla Trek

How to build a deeper first journey—and give Rwanda enough time to become more than one extraordinary encounter.

There is a moment that defines Rwanda for many first-time visitors.

You leave the cultivated hills behind, enter the cool forest on the slopes of the Virunga volcanoes, and somewhere ahead the trackers stop. Voices become quieter. The forest suddenly feels very still.

Then, through the vegetation, you see a mountain gorilla.

It is understandable that travellers cross continents for this encounter. Rwanda's mountain gorillas are extraordinary, and Volcanoes National Park deserves its place among Africa's great wildlife experiences.

But there is a mistake I often see in the way Rwanda is imagined from abroad: the gorillas become the entire journey.

They shouldn't be.

Rwanda rewards travellers who give it more time. Beyond the gorillas lies a remarkably varied country of volcanic landscapes, lakes, rainforest, savannah, wildlife, cities and communities. The best first journey is not a race towards one famous encounter; it is a journey that gives that encounter a country around it.

Begin in Kigali - and choose the part of the city that suits you

Kigali deserves more than a night between the airport and the national parks. Built across a succession of green hills and valleys, the capital is low-key by African-city standards but increasingly cosmopolitan, with a growing restaurant, cafe, art and design scene.

For a first visit, the neighbourhood you choose changes the feel of the stay.

Kiyovu is a strong choice for travellers who want to be central without feeling surrounded by the commercial centre. It is an established, leafy part of the city with convenient access to central Kigali and several well-known hotels. It works particularly well for a first or final night when you want the city close at hand but still want somewhere relatively calm.

Kimihurura gives you a more contemporary side of Kigali. Around the wider Kimihurura and convention-centre area are restaurants, cafes and places to spend an evening, making it a good base for travellers who want to experience the capital rather than simply sleep in it.

Nyarutarama is more residential and spacious. It suits travellers who prefer a quieter, more polished base and is close to the golf course and Nyarutarama Lake, where the walking track provides an unexpectedly peaceful pocket of urban nature and birdlife.

Whichever area you choose, allow time for the Kigali Genocide Memorial. It deserves to be approached as a place of remembrance and understanding, not fitted hurriedly between sightseeing stops. Then let the rest of the city show another side of Rwanda: galleries, local design, coffee, restaurants, markets and the everyday rhythm of a capital that has changed enormously.

I would give Kigali at least two nights on a first journey. One helps you recover from the flight. The second lets you begin understanding the country before heading into its landscapes.

Travel north slowly - the Virungas are more than gorillas

The road north towards Musanze and Volcanoes National Park is part of the experience. Cultivated hills rise and fall around you until the Virunga volcanoes begin to dominate the horizon.

The park's montane forest and bamboo habitat are famous for mountain gorillas, but they also support golden monkeys and remarkable birdlife. Golden monkey tracking makes an excellent second forest experience: faster, lighter and very different from the quiet intensity of meeting a gorilla family.

Musanze itself is useful as more than a gateway. Markets, local life and the surrounding volcanic landscape give travellers a reason not to arrive late, trek at dawn and immediately leave.

Give the gorilla experience the space it deserves

Gorilla trekking is not a conventional safari. There is no vehicle separating you from the landscape. You walk, sometimes over steep, wet or muddy ground, and the length of the trek depends on where the gorilla family is that day.

Good footwear and rain protection matter. So does using a porter. A porter makes the trek more comfortable and also creates direct local economic value around conservation tourism.

Most importantly, keep the afternoon after the trek relatively open. You have travelled a very long way for a limited, extraordinary period in the forest. Don't turn the experience into another appointment before rushing to the next hotel.

After the forest, change the rhythm at Lake Kivu

Lake Kivu provides a natural pause after the physical intensity of Volcanoes. The western landscape opens into water, islands, inlets and green hills, and towns such as Rubavu make the lake an easy continuation from the north.

This is where I would deliberately slow the itinerary. Take a boat or kayak onto the water, walk or cycle part of the lakeshore, or simply give yourself an afternoon with nowhere else to be.

For active travellers, sections of the Congo Nile Trail can be explored on foot or by bicycle. For everyone else, Lake Kivu's real value may be simpler: it creates space between Rwanda's headline experiences.

Go into the rainforest at Nyungwe

If the gorillas introduce you to Rwanda's primates, Nyungwe National Park expands the story dramatically.

One of Africa's oldest rainforests, Nyungwe is home to chimpanzees and an exceptional variety of other primates, including large black-and-white colobus groups, L'Hoest's monkeys, blue monkeys and vervets. The forest is also one of Rwanda's outstanding birding landscapes.

Chimpanzee tracking has a different energy from gorilla trekking. You may hear the animals before you see them, their calls carrying through the forest, and tracking can involve steep, slippery terrain. Even without a primate encounter, the scale and biodiversity of the rainforest make Nyungwe a nature experience in its own right.

For travellers who think Rwanda means only gorillas, Nyungwe is often the place that changes that perception.

See Rwanda's savannah side in Akagera

Then there is Akagera National Park in eastern Rwanda, where the landscape changes again: savannah, woodland, wetlands and lakes replace mountain forest.

Akagera is home to the Big Five following the reintroduction of lions and rhinos. On game drives, travellers can also encounter elephants, buffalo, giraffes, zebra, antelope, baboons and vervet monkeys. Leopards and hyenas are present too, although, as everywhere in the wild, sightings are never guaranteed.

The wetlands add another dimension. A boat trip on Lake Ihema can bring you close to hippos, Nile crocodiles and abundant waterbirds. That combination of savannah and wetland makes Akagera feel very different from the primate forests in the west and north.

This contrast is one of Rwanda's strengths. In one country, a traveller can move from a gorilla forest to a chimpanzee rainforest and then to a classic African savannah.

Think in nights, not checklists

Rwanda is compact, but that is not an invitation to hurry.

For a shorter first journey, Kigali and Volcanoes National Park already make a meaningful combination. Add Lake Kivu when you have another couple of nights and want a slower transition after trekking.

With more time, choose Nyungwe or Akagera according to what interests you most: rainforest and primates, or savannah and traditional safari wildlife. With enough time, both can create a remarkably varied journey.

The important thing is not to collect every national park. It is to give each landscape enough time to feel different from the one before it.

Don't add another destination simply because there is an empty day on the calendar. Add it because it changes the story of the journey.

The Rwanda you remember may not be the photograph you expected

Of course you will remember the gorillas.

You may remember the silverback turning towards you in the forest, or the almost human familiarity of a young gorilla playing nearby.

But Rwanda has a habit of leaving travellers with other memories too: morning mist between the volcanoes; a golden monkey disappearing through bamboo; chimpanzee calls somewhere inside Nyungwe; giraffes against Akagera's open horizon; a hippo surfacing beside a boat; coffee in Kigali; a quiet afternoon beside Lake Kivu.

These experiences do not compete with the gorillas. They give the gorilla encounter a country around it.

And that is the Rwanda I would encourage a first-time visitor to discover.

Come for the gorillas. But give yourself enough time to meet Rwanda.

A considered next step
